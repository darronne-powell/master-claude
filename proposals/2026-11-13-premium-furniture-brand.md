## Cover letter (copy-ready once the ⟨…⟩ parts are filled)

AI video is all I make, and furniture is a good fit for it: products that have to stay exactly right while the room, light and people around them change.

For your unboxings, room reveals and product swaps, I'd work like this:

1. Lock the product first. Clean reference frames of each piece from your images, with shape, fabric, wood finish and logo fixed, before any motion.
2. Build the scene around it. Room, warm natural light and characters as start/end frames, with a character sheet so the same person carries across videos.
3. Animate in short controlled shots. Higgsfield or Veo for realistic motion, Runway when a shot needs more camera control. Short shots drift less, which keeps the product accurate and saves credits.
4. Edit in CapCut. Hook in the first 2 seconds, pacing, music, voice-over sync, captions, and both 9:16 and 16:9 versions.

**My work with real products and people:** ⟨2–3 links from Darronne⟩

**Tools:** Higgsfield, Veo, Runway and Sora for video; HeyGen for presenters; CapCut for editing and AI voice-over. ⟨Image tool Darronne uses for product-in-room stills⟩. For an unboxing I'd pick Higgsfield or Veo: they handle hands and packaging most naturally.

**Keeping the design from drifting:** I never ask the model to invent the product. Every shot starts from a reference frame with the product already correct, shots stay short, and I check each frame against your images before it goes into the edit.
