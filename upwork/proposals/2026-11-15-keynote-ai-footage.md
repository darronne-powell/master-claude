## Letter

Consistency across 15 to 20 clips usually breaks at the hands, hairline and build before it breaks at the face, because each generation reinvents them when the outfit and lighting change.

If two clips in the reel look like slightly different people, an event planner notices, and the reel works against you.

"Not overly polished, cinematic, or obviously AI-generated" is the most useful line in your post. AI video tools default to a film look: shallow focus, slow push-ins, warm grading. Real conference footage looks different. It's a locked-off wide from the back of the room, a long lens that hunts a little, flat stage lighting and a cutaway to a nodding audience. I'd direct every clip toward that look.

How I'd approach yours:
1. Build a reference set from your photos and speaking footage: face, build, and how you move and gesture on stage.
2. Make 3 test clips in one venue and send them to you first. You approve the likeness, then we scale up.
3. Generate the rest venue by venue (small conference room, hotel ballroom, theater), changing outfit, stage setup and slide graphics, and checking each clip against the reference set.
4. Make the audience shots and reactions as separate clips, so you have cutaways for the reel edit.
5. Deliver each clip on its own at 1080p minimum, upscaled to 4K where the source holds up.

I work in Veo, Kling, Runway, Higgsfield and Sora, and pick the tool per shot, since some handle walking better and some handle faces under stage light better. I'm based in Florida, so your US requirement is covered.
