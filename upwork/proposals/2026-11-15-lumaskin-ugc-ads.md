## Letter

LUMA

Skincare is where AI UGC most often gives itself away: the models smooth the skin until it looks airbrushed, and the hands rubbing in a cream tend to melt into the jar. For a brand selling simple, effective skincare to real women, that plastic skin is the first thing a viewer notices, and the ad reads as fake before the hook lands.

You wrote that you don't want "obvious AI-generated videos." That tells me you've seen what most AI ads look like and how fast people scroll past them. So the work here is restraint: real skin texture, ordinary lighting, a phone-camera feel.

How I'd approach your three ads:
1. Build every shot from your product images as start frames, so the bottle, label and colors stay exact.
2. Keep skin texture, natural bathroom or bedroom light and a handheld look. Application close-ups stay short so nothing warps.
3. Write a different hook for each ad (a problem hook, a routine hook, a result hook), so you learn which angle works on Meta and which on TikTok.
4. Voice-over with lip-sync checked on every talking shot, then captions, sound effects and music, delivered at 1080x1920.

A free hook idea for one of the three:
Hook: "I have four minutes before school drop-off. This is my whole routine."
Middle: two products, quick close-ups, she's out the door.
End: "My skin has never looked this calm, and I barely try."
