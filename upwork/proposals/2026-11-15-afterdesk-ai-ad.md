## Letter

The part of an Afterdesk ad that AI video can't fake is the chat itself. Generators turn phone screens and message bubbles into blurred, half-readable text, and that's often the first "obvious AI artefact" people notice.

For you the chat is the product. If the viewer can't read "Booked. You'll get a confirmation text now," the ad shows a person looking at a phone and nothing more.

My fix: generate the people and places with AI, then put a real screen recording of the Afterdesk widget onto the phone in post, so every message is sharp and in your purple, pink and blue.

A free idea, 3 lines:
9:14pm. A plumber in his van eating dinner, phone face down. A customer's phone lights up instead.
On her screen, Afterdesk books her Tuesday 2:30pm slot while he finishes his meal.
End: "The front desk that never clocks off."

That comes straight from your homepage, which already tells the story well: someone lands on a site at 9pm and goes to whoever answers first.

How I'd approach yours:
1. Write the script and shot list for 30 seconds, built from 6 to 8 short clips.
2. Lock one character per role with reference images first, so faces stay the same in every shot. That's where most AI ads lose consistency.
3. Generate the clips, rerolling any shot with warped hands or faces.
4. Composite the real chat screens, then add voice-over, captions and music.
5. Deliver 9:16, 1:1 and 16:9 for Facebook and Instagram.

Tools: Google Veo, Kling, Runway, Sora and Higgsfield for video, Nano Banana and Midjourney for reference frames, and Premiere Pro, After Effects and CapCut for the edit.
