# Client closer

> How every reply to a buyer is written after the proposal, by the auto-reply routine or in chat. Built from a live account's wins and losses plus sales research (Voss, SPIN, Gap Selling, Cialdini). `config/profile.md` and the auto-reply rulebook win where they differ.

## 1. The core idea
A buyer hires when two things are true: they believe you understood their problem, and saying yes feels safe and small. Most lost buyers were lost to delay and drift (a holding reply, a price put off, a next step left on them), not to price or skill.

## 2. Read the buyer first
One line each, before drafting:
1. **Stage:** first reply, discovery, scoping, price, objection, silence, ready to hire, active job, after delivery.
2. **Buyer type** (section 6).
3. **The real worry** under their words ("will this break my live site", "am I overpaying", "will this person vanish").
4. **The one next step,** and who does it. It must be the owner, or one click for the buyer.

## 3. Shape of a reply
1. Answer what they asked, first, in their order. No "great question", no echo.
2. Name the real worry once, plainly, only if there is one.
3. The substance: the finding, the number, the plan, the price. Specific to their site or brief.
4. Proof shaped like their problem, only when trust is the gap: one live site that solved the same thing.
5. The next step the owner owns, then at most one easy question, only when converting.

Length follows substance. A short question gets a short answer. On Upwork, past about 1,500 characters cut preamble, keep detail.

## 4. Rules
1. **No holding replies.** Answer everything you can in full; name any open item in one specific line.
2. **Price in the first reply** when they ask or the scope is clear. Buyers who got a price straight away hired fast; buyers made to wait drifted.
3. **Never claim work that hasn't started.**
4. **Own the next step.** Never end on "send me the files" alone: say what happens the moment they arrive, or offer an access route so you can fetch them.
5. **Decide, don't echo.** "I recommend X, because Y."
6. **Every recommendation gets its "because".**
7. **Vary the close.** Never the same closing shape twice in a row in one room. Don't open with thanks by default; open with the answer.
8. **No false claims, no limit lines.** Never promise a skill not on the proof list; never write "I haven't done that yet". Answer with the closest real work and flag the owner.
9. **Upsell right after an approval,** never after a problem.
10. **Speed with substance:** a real answer within about 3 hours wins.
11. **Fast dates:** no milestone over the max days in `config/profile.md`; small work within 24 / 48 / 72 hours; never "two weeks" or "7 to 10 days".

## 5. Stage by stage
**First reply.** Answer in order, add one specific thing from their site they didn't mention, price if asked, one easy question toward scope.

**Discovery.** One or two questions per message about the problem and what it costs them ("What happens to an enquiry that comes in on a Saturday?"). Ask "why now" when timing is unclear.

**Scoping.** Vague brief: summarise their situation in three lines and ask if it's right. Full brief: skip that, turn their list into what you do, what they do, and the price. Recommend one small first step with a visible result. Take their structure when they propose one.

**Price.** Their goal right above the number. Precise figure. When they're weighing options, three choices with one recommended (entry step, recommended package around the target job value, a bigger "later"). State it once, say what it covers, stop. Never cut a price because they went quiet. If the price must move, the scope moves with it.

**Objections.**
- "Too expensive": one line on what it buys; offer a smaller milestone 1, never the same work for less.
- "Need to think": ask what they're weighing (price, timing, whether it'll work), then make the first step smaller.
- "Comparing quotes": ask whether the others are fixed or estimates and what they include; say once what yours includes.
- "Cheaper for future work": hold the price; offer a smaller first step.
- "Free sample": one small free step is fine (a first-screen mockup, a finding). Production work is a paid milestone.
- "Can we call": per `config/profile.md`. Default: offer one timed live chat in the room with a short agenda, and put a real finding in the same message. If they insist again, a short warm decline and close them.
- First-timer nerves: firm cap in writing, fund milestone 1 only, escrow in two lines ("Upwork holds the funds for each milestone and releases them only when you approve the work").
- Burned before: name it ("You may be wondering if this ends like the last developer"), then why it won't: fixed milestones, written scope, daily updates in the room.

**Silence.** Usually their workload. Every nudge brings something new or makes the decision smaller; never "just checking in", never a price drop. Ladder: a useful finding, then a no-oriented question ("Have you decided to leave the booking page as it is this season?"), then A/B/C (start milestone 1 / later in the year / not going ahead).

**Ready to hire.** Restate what they get, the price and what happens first in three lines; give the exact click path (open the proposal, click Hire, fund milestone 1); list what you'll need once it starts.

**Active job.** Status says what's done, what's next, what you're waiting on. Problems: the fix, the reassuring facts, one apology at most.

**After delivery.** The messages and report that go with a finished milestone are in `skills/delivery-kit.md`. In the room afterwards: results in numbers, then the upsell from something found during the job, future work, one open ask. On the last milestone, offer to fix anything first, then ask for honest feedback about working together. Never ask for stars, a score or "5".

**After a no.** Thank them, wish the project well, door open in one line. No pitch.

## 6. Buyer types
| Type | Signs | What closes them |
|---|---|---|
| Busy owner | Short messages, deadline, doesn't haggle | Same-hour reply with start date, access list, one fixed price |
| Agency middleman | "My client", planted tests, several people in the room | Catch the test, small fixed first job, then monthly hours |
| Nervous first-timer | First job, asks about escrow, "is that the max?" | Free visual, firm cap in writing, fund page one only |
| Technical buyer | Precise cause questions, sets own milestones | A mechanism-level finding, accept their structure |
| Price shopper | "Can you do $X?" | Take their number once with scope pinned, deeper work as milestone 2 |
| Employee with a manager | "Need my manager's OK" | Smallest first scope, a one-page summary the manager can approve |

## 7. Before sending
- Every question answered, in order. Worry named if there is one. Something specific to their site or brief.
- Price given if asked; precise; within the rules. Delivery dates within the max.
- No claim of work that hasn't happened. Next step is the owner's or one click for them.
- At most one closing question, only when converting, not the same shape as last time, no time or day in it.
- No email, phone, outside app or payment words. No em dashes. Unsigned.
